Aethoria · Materials What a realm can and cannot supply itself

Worked-Out Ground

I went looking for why city walls never appeared and found that they do — the fortification planner works. What does not work is the ground underneath it: a realm loses the ability to quarry its own stone and has no way to move a single block between its own settlements. Both measured from the live simulation.

A real fortification line, taken from a running world at year 80. Segments, corners, towers and gates are distinct roles, and the town keeps growing past its own wall.

Why it kept failing on materials

A line costs stone and timber together, so a realm short of either stops building. Neither fault below is a shortage of rock: one map still held 30,809 units of stone across 337 deposits while its capital had none.

Workings never came down Fixed

A quarry standing on ground it had already emptied kept its plot for the rest of the game. Production pins such a building's staffing to 0.15 rather than zeroing it, and the lifecycle reasons about employment collapse rather than about the map — so nothing ever reached the abandonment threshold.

One capital had 106 citizens, every plot spent, and a single quarry that had gone dry decades earlier, with 1,858 units of stone within walking distance. It could not open another anywhere, because it had nowhere to put one.

Production now records that the seam is what ran out, and the lifecycle weighs that heavily enough to retire the site on its own. Everything after is machinery the game already had: abandon, ruin, demolish, plot freed.

A realm could not supply itself Fixed

Trade routes exist only between kingdoms, and a route needs a price gap to clear the haul — which two cities of one realm can never have, because they share a market. So a capital with no stone had no way to buy from its own hamlet twenty tiles away, which produced 25 units a year and shipped none of them.

Realms now haul construction staples to their own short settlements. Because nearly every building in the game is part stone, this only ever moves a surplus — the numbers below are the whole mechanism.

Workings nobody can staff Held back

Extraction carries the weakest repeat penalty of anything, the same as housing, so camps of four open five quarries each. Across one realm, nineteen quarries stood on live deposits with exactly one staffed and producing. Labour, not rock, is what turns a seam into stone.

It is a real fault and I have a fix for it, but the only measurement I have is two seeds — where it also showed fewer cities and a third of the timber output. At that sample size that is indistinguishable from seed divergence, and this is the economy's most delicate coupling. It needs a proper multi-seed study before it ships.
